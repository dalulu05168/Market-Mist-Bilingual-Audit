Preface
Why I Wrote This Book

To Everyone Who Has Ever Been Lost in the Mist

I have seen too many intelligent people become unlike themselves in front of the market.

They think clearly in the boardroom and can break down a company's business model into more
than a dozen parts. They can read complex financial statements, explain the relationships
among interest rates, inflation, exchange rates, and industry cycles, and even persuade everyone
 at an investment conference with eloquent presentations.

Yet the moment prices begin to move rapidly across the screen, they become nervous, hesitant,
impulsive, and even irrational.

A single strong bullish candlestick can make them forget valuation.

A sudden bearish candlestick can make them forget logic.

A single news headline can make them abandon an investment plan they spent three months
preparing.

This is not because they are not intelligent enough.

On the contrary, many people fail in the market not because they lack intelligence, but because
they have never truly understood one thing:

Price is not the answer. Price is only the entrance to the mist.

When you see a stock rising, the truly important question is not:

“It has gone up. Should I buy it?”
But rather:

“Why is it rising?”

“Who is buying?”

“What happened before the advance?”

“Does volume confirm the move?”

“Is this a genuine breakout or a bull trap?”

“If I am wrong, where will the market tell me so?”
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 4 / 686>
Likewise, when you see a stock falling, the truly important question is not:

“It has fallen. Should I sell?”

But rather:

“Is this a broken trend or a healthy pullback?”

“Is this panic selling or institutional accumulation?”

“Has the decline broken below a key support level?”

“Does volume confirm the rebound?”

“Is this pattern preparing for the next trend, or completing a distribution phase?”

The greatest challenge in investment education is that the market never writes the answers
directly on the chart.

It only leaves clues—and those clues are often hidden in the mist.

Price is a clue.

Volume is a clue.

Support and resistance are clues.

Breakouts and retests are clues.

Top and bottom patterns are also clues.

The investor’s task is to learn how to read those clues through the mist.

I did not write this book to tell you about some magical indicator, nor to persuade you that any
particular chart pattern will always work. There is no pattern that works forever, and there is
no model that is always right.

What I truly want to share is a way of observing the market through the mist.

It begins with price, but does not stop at price.

It uses technical analysis, but does not worship chart patterns.

It studies fundamentals, but does not ignore timing.

It respects market psychology and understands the logic behind institutional capital.

Many years ago, at an international investment research firm, I met a young research analyst.

His name was Ethan Carter.

He was intelligent, hardworking, quick to learn, and he also had the most common weakness of
young investors: he was too impatient, too confident in himself, and too easily led by price.

He believed investing was about finding answers.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 5 / 686>
Later he realized that investing is more about asking better questions in the mist.

This book is the story of the journey that Ethan Carter and I experienced together through the
market mist.

You will see how he chased rising prices, panicked, misread chart patterns, became confused
despite excellent financial statements, grew overconfident in bull markets, doubted himself in
bear markets, and gradually learned to connect price, volume, chart patterns, capital flows,
sentiment, and risk.

After every story, I would bring him back to the whiteboard.

There, we did not discuss emotions—we discussed structure.

We did not argue about predictions—we examined evidence.

We did not search for perfect answers—we built better frameworks for judgment.

Because a truly mature investor is not someone who is always right.

It is someone who knows why they were wrong when the market proves them wrong, and knows
 why an opportunity exists when the market presents one.

That is what Market Mist hopes to accomplish.

It is not a cold technical analysis textbook.

Nor is it merely an inspirational investment story.

It is a classroom about the market.

And when you close this book, I hope you will remember not only Ethan Carter’s story, nor
merely a WBottom, Triple Top, Flag Pattern, or V-Shaped Reversal.

More importantly, I hope that whenever you see price again, you will ask one more question:

“What is really happening behind the mist?”
