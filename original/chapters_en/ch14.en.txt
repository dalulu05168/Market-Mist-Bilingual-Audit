Professor's Whiteboard Session

M Top / Double Top Pattern

That evening,

the subject of Ethan's whiteboard lesson was:

The M Top, also known as the Double Top Pattern.

The first thing I told him was:

        "Never assume a Double Top exists simply because you see two peaks."

That is the most common mistake traders make when identifying this pattern.

A true Double Top is not simply two peaks that resemble the letter M.

A true Double Top occurs when the market attempts twice to break above the same price area,

fails both times,

and then breaks below the support between those two peaks,

demonstrating that buying pressure has been exhausted

and sellers have gained control.



I. How a Double Top Forms

A standard Double Top usually develops through five stages.

Stage One: The Existing Uptrend

A Double Top almost always appears after a clearly established uptrend.

Without a prior advance,

the pattern carries far less significance.

Stage Two: Formation of the First Peak

Price rallies to a new high



                                                - 300 -
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 309 / 686>
before beginning to pull back.

The first peak is often accompanied by relatively heavy Trading Volume,

because market sentiment remains optimistic,

buyers are still enthusiastic,

yet selling pressure is beginning to emerge.

Stage Three: Formation of the Neckline

After declining from the first peak,

price eventually rebounds from a certain support level.

That low becomes the potential Neckline.

Stage Four: Failure of the Second Peak

Price rallies back toward the first peak, but fails to achieve a decisive breakout.

If Trading Volume during the second peak is lower than during the first,

it suggests weakening buying momentum.

If the second peak forms a long upper shadow,

shows expanding Trading Volume without further price progress,

or reverses sharply after testing the highs,

the warning becomes even more significant.

Stage Five: Neckline Breakdown Confirmation

Price declines from the second peak

and breaks below the Neckline between the two peaks.

Only then

is the Double Top officially confirmed.

Until the Neckline is broken, it remains nothing more than a potential Double Top.

II. How Does Trading Volume Confirm a Double Top?

Trading Volume provides some of the strongest evidence when evaluating a Double Top.

A healthy topping structure typically displays the following characteristics:




                                               - 301 -
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 310 / 686>
       The first peak forms on relatively heavy Trading Volume.
       The second peak forms on lighter Trading Volume.
       The Neckline Breakdown occurs on expanding Trading Volume.
       The Retest of the Neckline takes place on declining Trading Volume.

Why is lighter Trading Volume at the second peak so important?

Because it tells us that although price has returned to the previous high,

buyers are no longer as enthusiastic as they were during the first advance.

The market is still capable of pushing price back toward the highs,

but it no longer has enough buying pressure to produce a successful breakout.

That often signals that demand is beginning to weaken.

Meanwhile, when the Neckline is broken on expanding Trading Volume,

it indicates that selling pressure is being released aggressively.

III. The Two Sell Signals in a Double Top

When trading a Double Top,

there are generally two levels of sell signals.

The First Sell Signal: Failure of the Second Peak

When price fails during its second attempt to break above the previous high—

particularly if it forms a long upper shadow,

shows expanding Trading Volume without further price progress,

or develops on lighter Trading Volume than the first peak—

it may serve as an early warning to reduce exposure.

This is not the final confirmation.

It is an early risk warning.

At this stage,

investors may consider:

       Reducing position size.
       Locking in part of their profits.
       Avoiding new purchases near the highs.
       Waiting for confirmation through a Neckline Breakdown.

The Second Sell Signal: Neckline Breakdown

When price breaks below the Neckline between the two peaks,

accompanied by expanding Trading Volume,

the Double Top is officially confirmed.

This signal is far more decisive.

At this stage,

investors may consider:



                                                  - 303 -
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 312 / 686>
       Reducing positions further.
       Exiting swing-trading positions.
       Strengthening defensive risk management.
       Waiting for a failed Retest to confirm continued weakness.

If price fails to reclaim the Neckline after the breakdown,

the downside risk increases even further.

At that point,

the pattern should no longer be viewed as an ordinary pullback.



IV. How Is the Price Target for a Double Top Estimated?

The theoretical downside objective of a Double Top

is commonly estimated by measuring the distance from the peaks to the Neckline

and projecting that distance downward.

For example:

       Peak: €365
       Neckline: €310
       Height: approximately €55

After the Neckline Breakdown,

the theoretical measured target would be:

€310 − €55 = €255

This is not a precise prediction.

It is simply a method for estimating the potential downside risk.

In real markets,

price may never reach that objective.

Or it may decline far beyond it.

Fundamental conditions,

the overall market environment,

sector sentiment,

and investors' risk appetite

can all influence the final outcome.

Nevertheless,

the measured-move target serves one valuable purpose:

It reminds investors that once a major topping pattern has been confirmed,

the downside risk may be far greater than most people initially expect.

V. Common Mistakes When Trading a Double Top

Although the Double Top appears simple,


                                              - 305 -
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 314 / 686>
beginners frequently make four common mistakes.

Mistake One: Declaring a Double Top as Soon as Two Peaks Appear

Until the Neckline is broken,

the pattern remains only a potential Double Top.

Mistake Two: Ignoring Trading Volume at the Second Peak

If the second peak develops on strong Trading Volume

and successfully breaks above the previous high,

the Double Top has likely failed,

and the uptrend may continue.

Mistake Three: Assuming the Neckline Will Be Immediately Reclaimed

A rebound after a Neckline Breakdown is entirely possible.

However,

if the Retest occurs on light Trading Volume

and price fails to reclaim the Neckline,

the bearish signal becomes even stronger.

Mistake Four: Using Fundamentals to Dismiss a Technical Breakdown

Even outstanding companies

can form major market tops.

Excellent fundamentals do not guarantee that price will never decline.

Once the Market Structure has been damaged,

investors should respect the market's risk signals.

Chapter Trading Rules

That day,

I asked Ethan to write down six trading rules.

First, a Double Top usually forms only after a well-established uptrend.

Second, two similar highs do not confirm a Double Top. The pattern is confirmed only after price
breaks below the Neckline.

Third, when the second peak forms on lighter Trading Volume than the first, it is an important
warning that buying momentum is fading.

Fourth, the failure of the second peak is the first sell signal, while the confirmed Neckline
Breakdown is the second sell signal.

Fifth, if price fails to reclaim the Neckline after the breakdown, the former Support has become
new Resistance.

Sixth, even outstanding companies can form major market tops. Strong fundamentals should
never replace proper risk management.
